A Bit of History

The Story of the Tour

How It Started

In 2020, I was living in Washington D.C. and heard amazing things about the Georgetown walking ghost tours. But when I saw the price — $65+ per person — I thought, surely I can make my own!

So I did. I gathered ghost stories from online sources, shared them with friends, and each of us researched and presented a stop on our own DIY ghost walk. It was a total success, and way more fun (and affordable) than the real thing.

When I moved to Salt Lake City the next year, I brought the idea with me and kept it going.

The Salt Lake City Tour

Now in its sixth year, the SLC Walking Ghost Tour is a casual, community-led stroll through the city's most haunted corners: Capitol Hill, Central City, the Avenues, and beyond. Each year, my friends volunteer to research and present a new set of spooky stories, so the route and lineup are always changing.

Expect a friendly, not-too-scary evening full of history, local lore, and the occasional chill down your spine. It's not a professional tour, just a group of curious friends keeping Salt Lake's ghost stories alive.

We're always looking for new stops and storytellers! If you have a haunting to suggest, or want to lead a future stop, please reach out. Future routes may include the Salt Lake Cemetery, 9th and 9th, and Sugar House.

— Kyle Treasure

Before You Go

Frequently Asked Questions

Is it free?

Yes! I just put this on for my friends, so it's very casual.

Do I need to RSVP?

You will starting in 2026! Stay tuned for the 2026 sign up soon.

Where should I park?

I always recommend parking near the end of the route each year. That way it's easy to get back to your car after it's over.

How long does the tour take?

About 2 hours! But feel free to come and go as you please.

Is it appropriate for kids?

We've had kids as young as 7 years old join before... but it was slightly too scary for that poor kid. :-/ Ultimately it's up to you!

Is it accessible?

We'll stick to city sidewalks and roadways at all times, so it's as accessible as those are!

Why do you do this?

To get my friends together to walk around downtown and tell cool/fun/interesting stories.

Anything else I should know?

By joining this walking tour, you acknowledge that you are participating voluntarily and at your own risk. You understand that the tour involves walking on public streets and sidewalks, possibly at night, and that you are responsible for your own safety, belongings, and actions at all times.

You release the tour organizer and fellow participants from any liability for injury, loss, or damage that may occur during the event. You understand this is an informal, social activity among friends — not a commercial or professionally guided tour — and you agree to act responsibly and follow all traffic and safety rules.
